# Malaysia games-employment archive pass — 19 September 2026

## Decision

**No observations proposed.** This pass did not find an authoritative Malaysia-wide annual stock of people employed in game development. The available primary sources describe either (a) a combined digital-creative sector, (b) a regional, voluntary survey, or (c) vacancies/jobs created. Those measures cannot be converted to a national games-employment stock without an explicit allocation rule that the sources do not provide.

## Authoritative evidence located

* **MITI Report 2018, p. 71** reports that the *Digital Content & Creative Technology Industry* (animation; games and interactive media; film/TV VFX; new media/platforms) “created 11,590 jobs in 2018.” This is a jobs-created flow/cumulative claim, not a workforce stock, and it combines games with three other subsectors. The same page reports game-market/export figures but no games employment count.
* **Ministry of Digital press release, 30 January 2026** says the *digital creative industry* had “created over 11,000 high-value jobs” to date, while separately describing animation and digital games. Again: combined sector and created-jobs measure, not a games workforce stock.
* **MDEC SEA Game Industry Report 2021** is a regional survey, not a census. Phase 1 sampled 151 gaming-industry players across six countries; Malaysia represented 61% (about 92 respondents). It reports studio-size bins and Malaysian response distributions, never a Malaysia workforce total.
* **MDEC SEA Game Development Industry Talent Economics Report 2024** uses two regional surveys: 295 individual respondents and 48 studios across six countries. It profiles compensation/talent demand and studio manpower composition. It is not a nationally representative employment survey and supplies no Malaysia employment level.
* **MDEC SEA Digital Content Industry Talent Report 2022** mixes animation and games (its sample is 56% animation / 44% game development), with participation from six countries. It provides no Malaysia games workforce series.

## Edition trace and negative result

MDEC’s 2021 report identifies earlier editions: *Southeast Asia Game Industry Initiative Report 2015* and *Southeast Asia Game Industry Talent Report 2016*. The 2021 PDF says the 2016 edition studied supply and demand of talent; this bounded search did not recover an official first-party PDF/landing page for either old edition. No source visited supplied a usable, annual Malaysia games-only workforce stock.

## Exclusions

Do not use 11,590 (2018), 11,154 (MDEC 2025 reporting), “over 11,000” (2026), or MDEC’s occasional “10,000 jobs in employment” promotional wording as games employment. They cover digital creative/digital content aggregates and/or jobs created, and their boundaries differ. Do not treat the MDEC surveys’ Malaysia shares, role shares, or studio-size bands as national counts.


## Main-agent reconciliation

Main review: no accepted additions. MDEC 2021 and 2024 repeat baseline sources; 2022, MITI and later ministry evidence document exclusions, not national games employment.
