# Netherlands archive extension

The strongest missing primary observation is **3,000 Dutch games-industry jobs in 2011**, from *Games Monitor '12*, printed/PDF p. 28. It is an estimated employment stock, not FTE: LISA counts paid workplace jobs of at least 12 hours a week, then the authors supplemented unmatched firms from websites and a company survey and estimated 37 remaining firms. The scope includes qualifying applied and entertainment games, freelancers/self-employed persons and specialist services, and excludes foreign establishments, retail, gambling, education, government and clients whose game activity is not core business.

The publisher archive also provides two intermediate historical report vintages:

| 3,850 jobs, end-2018 | *Games Monitor 2018*, printed/PDF p. 10 | Preserve as the original report-vintage count. Do **not** overwrite the accepted 3,834 2018 row from the 2024 retrospective report. The 2018 report documents retrospective corrections to the 2015 base, demonstrating that discovery and classification changes can revise history. |
| around 4,000 employees, end-2020 | *Games Monitor 2020 Update*, printed/PDF p. 6; summary p. 8 | Proposed only as an approximate interim estimate. It relies on a limited survey and directory/website/LinkedIn checks, and says a detailed Monitor would follow. It is not a precise annual series point. |

The later archive confirms, rather than adds to, the accepted 2021 and 2023 observations. *Games Monitor 2022* reports **4,560 jobs at end-2021** at printed/PDF p. 14, and separately lists 1,302 jobs in applied-only firms and 145 in mixed applied-entertainment firms at printed/PDF p. 17. Those are components within the broad total, not a comparable entertainment-only national series. The accepted 2024-report employment observation remains **4,291 jobs at end-2023**. Its current applied-games table uses **FTE** wording (1,056 FTEs in applied-only companies and 74 FTEs in mixed companies), so it must not be spliced into the jobs headcount series or treated as a later total workforce point.

## Government and trade-source boundary

CBS National Accounts has annual jobs, employed-persons and FTE data by standard economic activity, but games are not a separately identifiable Dutch industry in the classification. *Games Monitor '12* explicitly says this is why it constructed a company-level list rather than use CBS/LISA aggregate industry tables. No government game-only annual employment series was located. The publisher-owned Games Monitor is therefore the decisive national source; CBS is a scope check, not an alternate series.

## Continuity limits and stopping point

The archive establishes report editions in 2012, 2015, 2018, 2020 update, 2022 and 2024. It does **not** establish annual observations between them. Later reports revise prior counts after discovering companies or refining which companies' core activity remains games; the accepted 2023 decline is also partly a focus/reclassification effect. Consequently, the defensible history is sparse report-vintage evidence, not an annual trend: 2011 estimated 3,000; 2015 accepted 3,030 (with its own imputation); 2018 accepted retrospective 3,834 plus original-vintage 3,850; 2020 approximately 4,000; 2021 accepted 4,560; and 2023 accepted 4,291.

The bounded pass stops here. No earlier game-specific national primary count predating 2011, and no later total national jobs observation after end-2023, was found in the primary publisher archive or CBS route. The remaining 2024 applied-games FTE components have a different unit and scope, so they are documented but not proposed as a total-employment continuation.
