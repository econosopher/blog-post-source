# China: provenance and employment-series review

## Answer to the congress question

The 2021-2025 decline plotted in the existing China packet comes from a **standalone CNG/Gamma Data report**, not the National People's Congress and not an identified release at the Chinese game industry's annual conference. Its PDF names **Beijing Gamma New Media Culture Communication Co., Ltd.** on p. 1, says on p. 2 that **Gamma Data (CNG) wrote the report**, and marks the chart data source as **Gamma Data (CNG)** on p. 8. A CNG-byline release dated 4 July 2025 says CNG released the report.

The exact source is *China Game Industry 2025 New Quality Productive Forces Development Report* / 《中国游戏产业2025「新质生产力」发展报告》. The full source is a 33-page report mirrored at [Eastmoney PDF](https://pdf.dfcfw.com/pdf/H3_AP202507181711204583_1.pdf?1752858662000.pdf=); provenance locators are pp. 1-2 and the panel is p. 8. [36Kr's CNG-byline release](https://www.36kr.com/p/3364490393409923) corroborates that it was released by CNG in early July 2025.

The similarly named official annual *China Game Industry Report* is a different product. Its 2025 bibliographic record identifies the Game Publishing Committee of the China Audio-Video and Digital Publishing Association (GPC) and the association's game committee as organizers, with CNG as strategic partner: [Social Sciences Academic Press record](https://cpms.ssap.com.cn/skwx_cmr/initDatabaseDetail?contentId=8531573&contentType=literature&siteId=23). That affiliation must not be carried across to the CNG-only p. 8 chart.

## What the plotted line measures

The chart title is **中国游戏产业主要上市游戏企业人员规模状况（万人）**: personnel scale of *major listed game companies* (ten-thousand people). It prints 22.30 (2021), 21.53 (2022), 20.65 (2023), 20.58 (2024), 20.34 (2024 H1), and 20.28 (2025 H1 E).

Its nearby narrative discusses cost pressure, optimizing team structure, focusing on core projects, shrinking non-core business, and AI. That supports only the report's claim about its selected listed-company panel. It does **not** establish an all-China industry workforce decline.

The report provides no constituent list, panel size, selection criteria, group-consolidation rule, geography of employees, game-revenue share threshold, segment allocation, or contractor treatment. It therefore cannot be relabelled as domestic game employment, and it cannot support a claim that a particular share of the decline was layoffs. The figures may contain overseas staff and non-game business staff and may also reflect changing company coverage. The panel has an 8.61% fall from 223,000 in 2021 to 205,800 in 2024, but that arithmetic is a source-panel change only.

Do not put the H1 values into an annual line. 2025 H1 carries an explicit **E** estimate marker.

## Earlier claim: recovered attribution, not a longer series

The 2018 claim of approximately 1.45m game-industry workers can be attributed more precisely than before: contemporary accounts tie it to CNG's talent/education research with Perfect World Education. [China News Service](https://www.chinanews.com/business/2018/10-29/8662582.shtml) reports their joint 2018 workforce/salary survey; [GameRes](https://www.gameres.com/833246.html) reports CNG's 28 December talent-education report and the approximate 1.45m figure; [Sina's release repost](https://k.sina.cn/article_2619343453_9c20025d01900jv65.html?from=game&subch=ogame) attributes the number to CNG statistics.

The original report and its population/counting method were not recovered. This is still a broad approximate estimate, not a census, and cannot be joined to the 2021-2024 listed-company panel. It is not sufficient to demonstrate national decline.

## Government and association search result

NBS has relevant classifications but no verified annual game-only employment series:

- [Culture and related industries classification](https://www.stats.gov.cn/xxgk/tjbz/gjtjbz/201805/t20180509_1758925.html) places work across internet game services (6422, including internet esports), animation and game digital-content services (6572), and only the relevant portion of application-software development (6513*).
- Its [Fourth Economic Census release](https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/zxfb/202302/t20230203_1900527.html) reports 2018 employment for the entire information-transmission/software/IT-services division: 9.951m total, 1.443m internet and related services, and 6.762m software/IT services. These are not game-only values.
- NBS's [2026 response](https://www.stats.gov.cn/hd/lyzx/zxgk/202609/t20260903_1965184.html) lists game-related activity only as part of a 16-industry cultural-new-form group. The published culture releases principally supply revenue and broad cultural-enterprise employment, not a repeatable game-only workforce total.

The official annual GPC/CADPA report archive was checked for employment wording. It provides industry-market reports and industry affiliation, but I found no inspected primary table with a national, game-only workforce total that meets the dataset's definition requirements.

## Integration recommendation

Keep China as `context_only`. Retain the CNG panel only as a clearly labelled company-panel inset and use this label verbatim: **"Personnel at major listed game companies (CNG panel; scope and geography undisclosed)"**. Do not say the decline came from "China," "China's game workforce," "the Chinese government," GPC, the National People's Congress, or a congress. Keep the 2018 1.45m claim in a separate provenance note; do not draw a connected 2018-2024 line.

## Follow-up: CNG listed/non-listed company reports

The related CNG *2026 China Listed/Unlisted Game Enterprise Competitiveness Report* adds a useful **definition for that report's own financial/market panels**, but no workforce sequence. Its CNG release hosted by [Jiemian](https://www.jiemian.com/article/14873199.html) says a “major listed game enterprise” is a Chinese listed enterprise in the **2025 game-revenue TOP50** whose game revenue is **more than 30% of total revenue**. It also says, to preserve consistency, its charts count only firms disclosing values in every corresponding period. This is valuable evidence that CNG's “major listed” metrics are a curated, disclosure-dependent corporate panel, not a national registry.

It cannot retroactively define the p. 8 new-productive-forces workforce chart: it is a different report with a different reporting year, and the p. 8 report does not state those rules. The 2026 release contains no aggregate personnel, employee, headcount, or R&D-headcount chart, so it supplies **no new observation**.

I also inspected the directly preceding available full reports: [2024 report, 68 pp.](https://30295522.s21i.faiusr.com/61/ABUIABA9GAAgsc_1tgYo0OD1iQU.pdf) and [2025 report, 78 pp.](https://download.s21i.co99.net/30295522/0/0/ABUIABA9GAAgzoC6xQYogLCAag.pdf?f=%E3%80%90%E4%BC%BD%E9%A9%AC%E6%96%B0%E5%AA%92%E6%96%87%E5%8C%96%E4%BC%A0%E6%92%AD%E6%9C%89%E9%99%90%E5%85%AC%E5%8F%B8%E3%80%912025%E4%B8%AD%E5%9B%BD%E4%B8%8A%E5%B8%82-%E9%9D%9E%E4%B8%8A%E5%B8%82%E6%B8%B8%E6%88%8F%E4%BC%81%E4%B8%9A%E7%AB%9E%E4%BA%89%E5%8A%9B%E6%8A%A5%E5%91%8A.pdf&v=1756266575). Neither contains a major-listed-company aggregate personnel/employee/headcount chart. They contain financial, game-market, product, company-case and sometimes company-specific R&D-staff material, which cannot be aggregated into a historical workforce series. The earlier-report route therefore adds no years and no compatible replacement for the existing contextual panel.
