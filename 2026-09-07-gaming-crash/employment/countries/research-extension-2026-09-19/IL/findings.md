# Israel games-employment archive — bounded research, 2026-09-19

## Result

No authoritative, published Israel-government source found in the bounded search provides a **domestic games-industry employment headcount**. Israel CBS does define computer-game publishing within software publishing (ISIC/Israeli classification 5820), but that class also covers operating systems and business software; it explicitly excludes software development (6201) and hosting (6311). It cannot represent the Israeli games ecosystem and no retrieved official table supplied a games-only headcount.

The traceable GameIS/Deloitte family supplies two useful **contextual ecosystem estimates**, but neither is safe as a domestic-workplace employment observation:

| Reference year | Headline | What it actually supports | Why it is not domestic employment |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| 2017 | approximately 4,000 workers / 170+ companies | a preliminary mapping of companies operating in Israel | The input is Start-Up Nation Finder plus GameIS; it deliberately includes cross-sector companies and supporting services (data analytics, AR/VR), and Deloitte says it did not independently verify inputs. No worker-location or FTE rule is stated. |
| 2021 | approximately 14,000 workers / approximately 200 companies (press material says 190) | a follow-on Israeli gaming-ecosystem estimate | No employment methodology or worker geography is published. The report treats ad-tech, monetization, analytics and supporting technologies as industry cornerstones; profiles cite global firms, offices and global workforces (e.g., Plarium’s 1,700 specialists across nine offices). |

Both reports include social-casino/casual activity. The 2017 report says it excludes games with monetary payouts, while 2021 includes 888 (online casino/sport/poker/bingo) in its selected list. This is a material definition break/ambiguity for any series intended to exclude real-money gaming.

## Primary excerpts retained

### 2017 Deloitte / Start-Up Nation Central, *Israeli Video Games Industry Report*

- PDF p. 8 (“A Note to the Reader”): “The primary source for the Israeli industry analysis was data obtained from the publicly accessible ‘Start-up Nation Finder’ … Additional information was received from GameIS …”; the data set “included companies whose target markets are not solely limited to the video games industry” because of supporting services such as “Data Analytics and AR/VR technology”; findings are “preliminary” and inputs were not independently verified.
- PDF p. 24: “over 170 companies, employing approximately 4000 workers”.
- PDF p. 5: social-casino-style games included are described as entertainment with “no monetary gains possible.”

### 2021 Deloitte / GameIS, *The Israeli Video Games Ecosystem* (published 2022)

- PDF p. 22: “~200 companies employing ~14,000 workers”; chart labels 2017 as 4,000 and 2021 as 14,000.
- PDF pp. 19–21: scope calls mobile/social/casual, monetization and supportive technologies (including ad-tech, AI/ML, AR/VR, crypto, performance marketing, AppsFlyer, ironSource, Overwolf and Singular) industry cornerstones.
- PDF p. 23: selected companies includes global companies operating in Israel; source note is Start-Up Nation, Crunchbase, Pitchbook and company websites. The Plarium profile states “more than 1,700 specialists” across headquarters and nine offices/studios, demonstrating that company profile staff figures are global, not Israel-only.
- PDF p. 25: selected list includes 888, an operator of casino, sport, poker and bingo brands.

## Family trace and negative results

- **Backward:** the 2017 Deloitte/Start-Up Nation Central report is the identifiable predecessor. It is not branded GameIS/Deloitte in the title, but cites GameIS as a secondary source and supplies the 4,000 baseline reproduced in 2021.
- **Forward:** the identifiable GameIS/Deloitte publication is the 2021-report release in July 2022. Searches for 2022–2026 GameIS employment reports found reuse of the 2021 headline and a 2023 GameIS/Women in Gaming survey on women’s shares in R&D teams, not a new total workforce count. The WN Conference 2022 survey lists company-type estimates but explicitly says nobody knows the exact number; it reports no total employees.
- **Government:** searched gov.il, Israel Innovation Authority and CBS material. Retrieved official outputs are high-tech/ICT or broad software categories, not an explicit games-industry workforce. CBS classification 5820 is too broad and does not cover the development/support activities included by GameIS/Deloitte. Do not substitute broad high-tech employment.

## Recommended treatment

Keep the two proposed rows as `context_only` and label their geography as **Israeli ecosystem / companies operating in Israel; worker location unresolved**. Do not present 4,000 → 14,000 as domestic Israeli jobs, FTEs, employees on Israeli payroll, or a real-money-gaming-excluded series. A usable domestic series would need a respondent/company register that reports Israel-based workers and separately flags publishing/development, support/ad-tech, and real-money gaming.


## Main reconciliation

Both workforce observations duplicate the original baseline. No new accepted observations. The independently read 2017 methodology confirms broad supporting-services coverage and unverified preliminary inputs.
