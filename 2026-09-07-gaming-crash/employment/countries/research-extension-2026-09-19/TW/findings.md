# Taiwan game-industry employment archive: bounded source pass

## Decision

Retain two **game-only, observed employee-count** records as distinct survey observations:

| Reference year | Value | What it covers | Source |
|---|---:|---|---|
| 2019 | 9,047 people | Employees at 98 game-industry companies: 46 developers and 52 agents/publishers. | TAICCA, *2020 Taiwan Cultural Content Industry Survey Report, Vol. 4* |
| 2022 | 9,929 people | Employees of the 133 game companies surveyed; company nonresponses were supplemented from 104 Job Bank public records where available. | TAICCA, *2022 Taiwan Cultural Content Industry Survey Report: Games and Immersive Interactive Games* |

These are not a harmonized national total or FTE series. Label them `surveyed-company employees`; do not join them into a time series without a methodology-break note.

## Primary findings

### 2019: game-only observation

The 2020 TAICCA cultural-content report states that 98 game companies employed 9,047 people: 46 game-development companies employed 5,062 people and 52 agency/publishing companies employed 3,985 people. It reports esports separately (19 companies, 660 employees), so **do not add esports to the game observation** unless creating an explicitly combined series. The report's 2019 game revenue estimate is also based on these 98 companies, supporting 2019 as the reference period.

### 2022: game-only observation

The 2022 report says its survey list targeted 200 companies, with 133 ultimately surveyed; it records 9,929 employees among responding companies. For employee counts, companies that did not provide an open response were backfilled from 104 Job Bank public information where available; unknowns remained "no data." The report additionally says its roster favoured companies active in the prior year and excluded paused/dissolving firms, warning that results may be optimistic. This is an observed/reconstructed respondent-frame total, not national employment.

## Scope boundaries

* **Do retain:** game development; operation, agency and publishing; game distribution/channel, physical/virtual exhibition platforms, and game-media businesses when included in the TAICCA survey frame.
* **Do not merge by default:** esports. The 2019 report separately counts it; newer reports are titled games and esports but their presentation may mix analytic context without offering a compatible employment total.
* **Do not substitute:** MODA/MOEA `digital content` employment, which covers multiple subsectors (games, animation, audiovisual, digital learning/publishing, immersive technologies) and is broader than games.
* **Do not substitute:** amusement/theme-park, arcade, esports venue, general software, or tax-industry counts. They represent different activity definitions.
* **Not FTE:** both retained values are employees/headcount-style counts. Neither source states FTE conversion or a payroll-census basis.

## Backward/forward coverage result

* **Backward:** found a 2019 game-only survey observation. A 2004-era academic labour-demand study and later MOEA digital-content material are useful history but combine digital-content subsectors and/or forecast demand; no additional comparable game-only official headcount was retained.
* **Forward:** the Ministry of Culture published a newer game/esports report (2024 publication) with game-company and employee-composition material, but this bounded pass did not locate a directly stated, comparable total employee count in the searchable text. Treat as a follow-up source, not a zero or missing value.
* **Recurring official industry-yearbook route:** TCA's official download portal confirms annual Digital Content Industry Yearbooks (MOEA through 2021, MODA thereafter). Their stated remit is broad digital content and the accessible catalogue/contents do not establish a game-only employment series. Use them for definitions and industry context only unless table-level extraction identifies a compatible game headcount.

## Recommended use

Use the two records as citations about the surveyed frames in their reference years. If a chart needs a single country series, leave 2020-21 and 2023+ unpopulated until a compatible government administrative dataset or report-table reconciliation is obtained. Do not interpolate or convert either count to FTE.
