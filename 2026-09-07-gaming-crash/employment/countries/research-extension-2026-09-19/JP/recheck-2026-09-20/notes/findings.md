# Japan authoritative-employment recheck

## Decision

No new chart-eligible, dated national workforce observation was found outside the existing Economic Census and METI routes. The checked public materials did not yield a comparable CESA employment series; this does not establish that one is absent from paid, archived or unindexed reports.

The dated national game-software workforce counts verified for this chart remain the Economic Census establishment-class observations already accepted:

| Reference date | Count | Scope and geography | Primary locator |
|---|---:|---|---|
| 1 February 2012 | 9,452 persons engaged; 246 establishments | Japan, JSIC 3914 `Game software industry`, establishment tabulation | [2012 Economic Census service-industry results](https://www.stat.go.jp/data/e-census/2012/kakuho/pdf/service.pdf), Table I-1, national 3914 row |
| 1 June 2016 | 18,216 persons engaged; 256 establishments | Japan, JSIC 3914 `Game software industry`, establishment tabulation | [e-Stat 2016 Service Industries B detailed-industry Table 1-1](https://www.e-stat.go.jp/dbview?sid=0003215380), national `3914 ゲームソフトウェア業` row |

These are domestic establishments classified as game-software businesses. They are not total Japanese games-company employment, a developer occupation count, or an FTE measure. The 2016 official download’s national 3914 row explicitly contains `256, 18216`; the project’s prior acceptance record establishes the matching 2012 row and table restrictions. Neither count should be joined to CESA ecosystem estimates, CEDEC respondent samples, or METI’s different enterprise-survey universe.

## CESA annual publications

CESA’s annual publication is useful context; the public material checked in this pass did not yield an eligible historical workforce series.

- The official [CESA Game Industry Report 2024 page](https://www.cesa.or.jp/action/industry-research/2024/) says the White Paper was comprehensively revised after 2023; it lists a 20 December 2024 release, a 360-page paid book/PDF, and describes total game-related industrial population as around 200,000. It supplies neither an employment reference date nor a reproducible public scope/method for that number. It cannot be plotted as a 2024 stock.
- The [CESA Game Industry Report 2026 preview announcement](https://www.cesa.or.jp/information/info6/001290/cesa_2026.html), published 17 September 2026, says the preview is distributed to media at Tokyo Game Show and the complete paid report is due in December. The linked [press release](https://www.cesa.or.jp/uploads/cesa_report_preview_0914.pdf), pp. 1-3, says the report contains employment-condition data but publishes no workforce total. It reports 48 valid responses from 220 CESA member companies (June-July 2026) for the company AI survey and 1,349 responses for the developer survey, including educators and students. These are survey samples, not national workforce stocks.
- The paid 2024 report’s list price is JPY55,000. No purchase or non-public access was attempted.

CESA's prior annual White Paper line was a home-console-game-industry publication; its public releases do not establish a dated all-games workforce series. The 2024 redesign is an additional method-break boundary, so even a future report total cannot be backfilled into the White Paper years without explicit comparable historical data.

## CEDEC developer surveys

CESA/CEDEC does maintain annual developer surveys, but they measure the respondent population rather than national employment. The public [2025 report](https://www.cesa.or.jp/uploads/2026/info20260106-2.pdf), pp. 4-5, states it is an internet survey conducted 2 June-4 August 2025, with 339 valid responses. Its stated target is primarily commercial game developers but includes educators and students. The report is therefore valid for labelled respondent shares and workplace characteristics only, never for a workforce count or national trend.

## Why the chart is sparse

Japan is not excluded. The available public sources answer different questions:

1. Economic Census has a dated, national, detailed industry-class workforce but only the recovered 2012 and 2016 `3914` observations; the inspected 2021 public results do not provide an equivalent detailed worker row.
2. CESA has annual industry publications, but their public materials supply undated broad estimates, paid full editions, or sample surveys rather than annual, comparable national workforce totals.
3. CEDEC has recurring developer surveys, but voluntary/respondent sample sizes are not industry headcount.

The defensible presentation is consequently a two-point 2012/2016 census series with an explicit data gap, while retaining CESA and CEDEC only as clearly labelled context. Do not interpolate, use the 2024/25 CESA ~200,000 figure as an annual observation, or substitute broader software/information-industry totals.

## Access limits and next checks

- CESA 2026 preview is a 30-page non-sale event distribution; the full approximately 460-page report is scheduled for December 2026. Its public press release does not disclose a workforce total.
- CESA 2024 full report is paid. Its public page establishes the report’s availability and contextual claim only.
- The next legitimate extension route is a public Economic Census detailed-class worker table matching JSIC 3914 and the prior establishment-tabulation restrictions, not a CESA estimate or a blocked METI form 5301 route.
