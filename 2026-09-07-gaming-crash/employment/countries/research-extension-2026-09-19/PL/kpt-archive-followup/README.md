# Poland: KPT / Digital Dragons archive

Recovered original 2017 report from the CD PROJEKT corporate mirror after the publisher archive's old download returned 404. Recovered the 2020 edition from KPT's current app/uploads route; its filename says 2021, but this is the report titled Kondycja Polskiej Branży Gier 2020. Upload dates and filenames are not employment reference dates.

## Methods and usefulness
- **2015:** publisher-linked PDF web text, p4: Intermarum anonymous online survey of 41 game producers, sent to decision makers. Separate gamer study has 3,750 respondents; those are consumers, not employees. Direct PDF download returned 404; web-indexed methodology retained as partial access only.
- **2017:** full original PDF p5: developer survey December 2016-March 2017, 101 respondents from approximately 311 identified firms; modified Intermarum questionnaire. A separate SPG/Advisory Group TEST pay study covers more than 1,100 workers and reports 2016 gross pay. Its claim of approximately 22% sector coverage is not a reproducible national employment estimator.
- **2017 p72:** firm-size chart explicitly counts people on employment contracts (umowa o prace). Narrative says workforce size is difficult to establish and interviews suggest civil-law contracts cover roughly three quarters of workers. Do not multiply size-bin midpoints or scale the pay sample into a national total. The chart's employment-contract scope is materially different from broad people-in-production counts.
- **2020 edition p113:** developer fieldwork September-October 2019, contact frame 476 verified digital-game producers, 34 questions and 100 complete questionnaires, plus 18 structured interviews. Separate manaHR pay/personnel study collected August-September 2020, valid at 31 July 2020: 2,241 workers, including 1,700 in direct game development and the rest support roles; sample balanced to reduce large-company influence.
- **2020 edition p122:** firm-size and preferred-contract distributions come from Polish GameDev 2019. Contract preferences are percentages of firms, not percentages of workers. Do not interpret 57% civil-law / 24% employment / 19% B2B as a national workforce split.

No new national annual observation accepted. These sources deepen historical coverage but do not validate the GIC retrospective 4,000 (2016) / 6,000 (2018) estimates as a consistent series. Retain the independent research families and sample dates.
