# Brazil

Abragames provides developer-workforce estimates for 2018, 2022 and 2023, including partners and workers at formal and informal developers. Outsourcing and foreign-worker figures exist only for changing employer samples. No newer verified national workforce observation was found by cutoff; the 2024 white paper repeats the 2023 estimate.

Coverage: usable_series. Research cutoff: 9 September 2026.

## Definitions

### Estimated people at Brazilian game developers (BR_developer_people)

Unit: people. Population: national_estimate. Geography: unknown.

- **industry scope:** Brazilian digital-game developers, including own games, work-for-hire/co-development and serious games; formal and non-formal firms
- **occupation scope:** All roles in covered businesses
- **geography note:** Brazilian developer organisations; worker residence and offshore remote employment not separately established. Includes foreign-owned developers operating in Brazil.
- **worker status:** Partners/founders and collaborators at formal and non-formal developers
- **contractor treatment:** Outsourced workers appear in developer workforce; separate standalone freelancer survey not added to this total.
- **nationality definition:** Not reported
- **method:** Firm mapping and respondent mean employment for formal/non-formal groups, extrapolated by report authors to mapped developer universe.
- **sample size:** 2018 employment table: 201 formal +57 informal respondents; 2022:164+30; 2023:220+34.
- **comparison note:** Discrete survey estimates with changing mapped coverage and respondent composition. 2018 historical chart contradicts table; use table 4,234 confirmed in 2022 and 2023. Not FTE or payroll-only national employment.

## Limits

- No 2015-2017, 2019-2021 or post-2023 national stock estimates verified. Missing years remain blank.
- Developer company workforce includes founders, informal and outsourced work; it is not payroll headcount or FTE. Individual work location not established.
- 2023 Table7 gives 2018=4,234 while Figure18 says5,292; prior 2022 Table5 confirms4,234. 2014 is similarly inconsistent and outside requested period.
- Standalone freelancer survey cohort is separate and cannot be added to developers without deduplication.
- Nationality and contractor samples have different question denominators and cannot be converted to national shares.
- IBGE administrative classification page was blocked on retrieval; industry primary report describes why CNAE codes fail to isolate game development.
